Determining Motion I [in Classical Mechanics (古典力学)]

IGS 200  · AY2026 Spring ·  Tachibana (GC)

The Question

When can we say
  • a motion is fully determined, (運動を完全に定める) or that
  • a given motion is completely known? (与えられた運動を完全に知る)

Determining Motion

Setup: motions of a point (点)

Why simplify to a point?   To strip away internal structure — no rotation, no deformation, no internal degrees of freedom. The motion is then captured by a single position over time.

The position changes along a line. If we lay out time horizontally, the same motion becomes a curve in the (t, x)-plane:

If you can draw this x(t) curve, you know every kinematic quantity of the motion.

When can you draw this graph?

You need to know the value of x (position) for all t (time). Mathematically, \(x(t)\) is a function of \(t\) (関数); we need its functional form (関数形).

Example: \(x(t) = t + 1\)

You can tell the location of P at every time.

Velocity (速度)

Can \(x(t)\) tell the speed or velocity of the motion?

Yes! But how?

Reminder: velocity is a signed quantity (in 1D the sign indicates direction); speed is its magnitude: \(\;\text{speed} = |\text{velocity}|.\)

Start with constant velocity \(v\) (速度一定)

The slope (傾き) of the line equals the velocity \(v\).

For a non-linear curve, the velocity changes over time. Zoom in on a small interval near time \(t\): the curve is well-approximated by the chord from \( (t, x(t)) \) to \( (t+\Delta t,\; x(t+\Delta t)) \).

where   \(\Delta t = (t+\Delta t) - t,\quad \Delta x = x(t+\Delta t) - x(t)\).

The slope of this chord approximates the velocity:

\[ v_{\text{approx}} \;=\; \text{slope} \;=\; \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} \;=\; \frac{x(t+\Delta t) - x(t)}{\Delta t} \]

Both \(x(t+\Delta t)\) and \(x(t)\) are known once the function \(x(t)\) is known.

Reproducing the original curve

Replace the curve by line segments of width \(\Delta t\). The smaller \(\Delta t\), the better the polyline matches the curve at every point.

Take the limit   \(\boxed{\;\Delta t \to 0\;}\)   to recover the curve exactly.

In the limit, the chord becomes the tangent line (接線), whose slope is the instantaneous velocity:

\[ \boxed{\;v(t) \;=\; \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{x(t+\Delta t) - x(t)}{\Delta t} \;=\; \frac{dx(t)}{dt} \;=\; x'(t)\;} \]

derivative of \(x\)  (微分)

This is defined as the true velocity of the motion.

Summary

  • Velocity is the derivative of \(x(t)\) (微分), thus
  • Velocity can be obtained from \(x(t)\) by differentiation.
\[ v(t) \;=\; \frac{dx(t)}{dt} \;=\; \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{x(t+\Delta t) - x(t)}{\Delta t} \]

Example

Let \(x(t) = (t-1)^2 - 1\). Find the velocity at \(t = \tfrac{5}{2}\).

\[ v(t) \;=\; \frac{dx}{dt} \;=\; \bigl[(t-1)^2 - 1\bigr]' \;=\; (t^2 - 2t)' \;=\; 2t - 2 \] \[ v\!\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right) \;=\; 2 \cdot \tfrac{5}{2} - 2 \;=\; 5 - 2 \;=\; \underline{\,3\,} \]

From velocity back to position

Velocity is obtained by differentiating \(x(t)\). Can we recover \(x(t)\) from the functional form of velocity \(v(t)\)?

▶ Integration is the inverse operation of differentiation (積分 — 微分の逆操作):

\[ \int_{a}^{b} \frac{dx(t)}{dt}\,dt \;=\; \bigl[\,x(t)\,\bigr]_{a}^{b} \;=\; x(b) - x(a) \]

(this is the fundamental theorem of calculus)

Only the displacement \(x(b) - x(a)\) can be obtained from the integral. To know the location \(x(b)\) itself, you still need to know \(x(a)\):

\[ x(b) \;=\; \bigl(x(b) - x(a)\bigr) + x(a) \;=\; \int_{a}^{b} v(t)\,dt \;+\; \underline{\,x(a)\,} \]

Summary

  • Displacement is the integral of \(v(t)\).
  • To determine the location, knowing the velocity \(v(t)\) is not enough — you also need an initial position.
\[ x(b) - x(a) \;=\; \int_{a}^{b} v(t)\,dt \]

Example

Given \(v(t) = 2(t-1)\) and \(\boxed{\,x(1) = -1\,}\), find \(x(t=2)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} x(2) - x(1) &= \int_{1}^{2} v(t)\,dt = \int_{1}^{2} (2t - 2)\,dt \\ &= \bigl[\,t^2 - 2t\,\bigr]_{1}^{2} = (2^2 - 2\!\cdot\!2) - (1^2 - 2\!\cdot\!1) \\ &= 0 - (-1) \;=\; 1 \end{aligned} \] \[ x(2) \;=\; x(1) + 1 \;=\; -1 + 1 \;=\; \underline{\,0\,} \]
