Determining Motion II [in Classical Mechanics (古典力学)]

IGS 200  · AY2026 Spring ·  Tachibana (GC)

1. Review (復習)

  • Determining motion = determining the functional form (関数形) of the location \(x(t)\) = knowing the value of the location at every time \(t\).
  • Velocity (速度) is the derivative (微分) of \(x(t)\): \(\;v(t) = \dfrac{dx(t)}{dt}\), so it can be obtained solely from \(x(t)\).
  • Displacement is the integral of \(v(t)\): \[ \int_{a}^{t} v(t')\,dt' \;=\; \int_{a}^{t} \frac{dx(t')}{dt'}\,dt' \;=\; \bigl[\,x(t')\,\bigr]_{t'=a}^{t'=t} \;=\; x(t) - x(a). \] Therefore, if \(x(a)\) is known, one can obtain the location \(x(t)\) as \[ \boxed{\;x(t) \;=\; x(a) \;+\; \int_{a}^{t} v(t')\,dt'\;} \]

[Example 1]

Let \(x(t) = t^3 + t^2 + t + 1\). The velocity is

\[ v(t) \;=\; \frac{dx(t)}{dt} \;=\; 3t^2 + 2t + 1. \]

In particular, at \(t = 1\),

\[ v(1) \;=\; 3 + 2 + 1 \;=\; \underline{\,6\,}. \]

[Example 2]

Let \(\;v(t) = t^3 + t^2 + t + 1\;\) and suppose the location at \(t = 0\) is \(x(0) = 1\).

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t) &= x(0) + \int_{0}^{t} v(t')\,dt' \\[2pt] &= 1 + \left[\,\tfrac{1}{4}t'^4 + \tfrac{1}{3}t'^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}t'^2 + t'\,\right]_{t'=0}^{t'=t} \\[2pt] &= 1 + \tfrac{1}{4}t^4 + \tfrac{1}{3}t^3 + \tfrac{1}{2}t^2 + t. \end{aligned} \]

In particular, at \(t = 1\),

\[ x(1) \;=\; 1 + \tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{2} + 1 \;=\; \frac{12 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 12}{12} \;=\; \underline{\;\tfrac{37}{12}\;}. \]

Acceleration (加速度)

The acceleration is the derivative of velocity:

\[ \boxed{\;a(t) \;=\; \frac{dv(t)}{dt} \;=\; \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{dx(t)}{dt}\right) \;=\; \frac{d^{2}x(t)}{dt^{2}}\;} \]

As with the position-velocity relation, going the other way uses integration. Given the acceleration and an initial velocity \(v(t_0)\),

\[ v(t) \;=\; v(t_0) \;+\; \int_{t_0}^{t} a(t')\,dt'. \]

The equation of motion (運動方程式)

What causes acceleration? In classical mechanics the answer is Newton's second law:

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\;m\,a(t) \;=\; F\;}\)    \(m\) : mass (質量), \(F\) : force (力)

Equivalently,

\[ a(t) \;=\; \frac{F}{m}. \]
Acceleration is driven by force (力がかかると加速度が生じる).
In a physics problem we determine the motion of an object when a force is applied to it (力がかかっている時の運動を決定する).

In a gravitational field (重力場)

Near the Earth's surface, every object of mass \(m\) experiences a downward gravitational force. Choose the \(x\)-axis to point upward; then the force is

\[ F \;=\; -m g, \qquad g \;\approx\; 9.8\ \text{m/s}^{2}. \]

Substituting into the equation of motion,

\[ m\,a(t) \;=\; -m g \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;a(t) \;=\; -g\;} \]

i.e. a constant acceleration (定数), independent of the mass \(m\).

[Example 1]

A particle is released from rest at height \(h\). Setting \(t_0 = 0\),

\[ x(0) = h, \qquad v(0) = 0, \qquad a(t) = -g. \]

Integrate the acceleration to get the velocity:

\[ \begin{aligned} v(t) &= v(0) + \int_{0}^{t} a(t')\,dt' = 0 + \int_{0}^{t} (-g)\,dt' \\ &= \bigl[\,-g\,t'\,\bigr]_{0}^{t} = -g\,t. \end{aligned} \]

Integrate the velocity to get the position:

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t) &= x(0) + \int_{0}^{t} v(t')\,dt' = h + \int_{0}^{t} (-g\,t')\,dt' \\ &= h - \tfrac{1}{2}g\,t^{2}. \end{aligned} \]

Time to hit the ground   (着地時刻)

The ball lands when \(x(t^{*}) = 0\):

\[ h - \tfrac{1}{2}g\,t^{*\,2} = 0 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; t^{*} \;=\; \sqrt{\,\tfrac{2h}{g}\,}. \]

The speed at impact is

\[ |\,v(t^{*})\,| \;=\; g\,t^{*} \;=\; \sqrt{\,2gh\,}. \]

[Example 2]

The ball is thrown up from ground level with initial speed \(v_0\). Setting \(t_0 = 0\),

\[ x(0) = 0, \qquad v(0) = v_0, \qquad a(t) = -g. \]

Velocity:

\[ v(t) \;=\; v(0) + \int_{0}^{t}(-g)\,dt' \;=\; v_0 - g\,t. \]

Position:

\[ \begin{aligned} x(t) &= x(0) + \int_{0}^{t} v(t')\,dt' = 0 + \int_{0}^{t} (v_0 - g\,t')\,dt' \\ &= \left[\,v_0\,t' - \tfrac{1}{2}g\,t'^{\,2}\,\right]_{0}^{t} = v_0\,t - \tfrac{1}{2}g\,t^{2}. \end{aligned} \]

Completing the square reveals the parabola's vertex:

\[ x(t) \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{2}g\!\left(t - \tfrac{v_0}{g}\right)^{\!2} + \frac{v_0^{\,2}}{2g}. \]

The maximum height \(\,x_{\max} = \dfrac{v_0^{\,2}}{2g}\,\) is reached at \(\,t_{\text{top}} = \dfrac{v_0}{g}\,\), and the ball returns to the ground at \(\,t_{\text{return}} = \dfrac{2v_0}{g}\,\) by symmetry.

9. Summary

  • Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, equivalently the second derivative of position: \(\;a(t) = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = \dfrac{d^2 x}{dt^2}.\)
  • The equation of motion is Newton's second law: \(\;m\,a(t) = F\). Force drives acceleration.
  • In a gravitational field the force is \(F = -mg\), so \(a(t) = -g\) — a constant, independent of mass.
  • Two integrations give \(v(t)\) then \(x(t)\). Two initial conditions \(x(0)\) and \(v(0)\) are required to determine the motion completely.
